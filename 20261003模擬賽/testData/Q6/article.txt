Learning to type well is a skill that pays off for the rest of your life
Every program you write and every message you send begins with your fingers on the keys
When you first start you may feel slow and you may look down at the keyboard after every letter
That is normal and every good typist once felt the same way

The secret is to keep your fingers resting on the home row
Your left hand sits on the letters A S D and F while your right hand sits on J K and L
From this position each finger only needs to move a short distance to reach the other keys
Small bumps on the F and J keys help you find the home row without looking down

Accuracy is more important than speed at the beginning
If you rush and make many mistakes you will build bad habits that are hard to break
Type slowly and carefully until each word feels natural under your hands
Speed will come by itself after many days of steady practice

Good posture also matters more than most people think
Sit up straight and keep your feet flat on the floor
Let your wrists float above the desk and keep your eyes on the screen instead of your hands
Take a short break every hour to rest your eyes and stretch your arms

Programmers spend many hours each day writing code and reading documents
A fast and accurate typist can turn ideas into working programs much more quickly
In a contest every second counts and a few extra words per minute can make a real difference
So practice a little every day and you will be surprised by how far you can go
